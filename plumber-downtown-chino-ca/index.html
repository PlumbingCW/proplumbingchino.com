Where downtown Chino is and how it started

The City of Chino General Plan places downtown on Central Avenue, with the core between Sixth Street and Central Avenue, south of C Street. It began in the late 1800s near the Southern Pacific Railroad depot, and the city's historic buildings, including those along D Street, cluster in this part of town. The Chino Transit Center at 6th Street and Chino Avenue is a useful landmark.

Richard Gird subdivided the old Rancho Santa Ana del Chino in the 1880s, and the town was incorporated in 1910. The practical takeaway is simple: buildings downtown were built across many decades, so two neighbors can have very different pipes behind the walls.

Homes and small businesses on the same blocks

Downtown mixes smaller single-family homes with storefronts and restaurants. A home usually needs a leak found or a drain cleared. A small business needs the restrooms, kitchen drains and water heater working during its open hours. Businesses can ask about commercial plumbing, and homeowners about residential service.

For a repeat clog, ask for a camera check instead of a second cleaning. If supply pipe is failing in several places, repiping may come up as an option, and it is worth comparing against repairing each leak.

Permits for older buildings

Work on older buildings can bring permit questions that newer homes do not. The City of Chino Building Division is at 13220 Central Avenue and can be reached at (909) 334-3251. Ask before you start a bigger project, and keep any written findings from the plumber.

Before you call from Downtown Chino

1. Find the shutoff

Locate the main valve. In a mixed building, ask which shutoff serves your space.

2. Note the symptoms

Write down which drains are slow and when, so the plumber can narrow the cause.

3. Clear the area

Move belongings away from wet floors and cabinet bases.

4. Keep records

Save photos and any inspection recording for permits, a sale or an insurance claim.
