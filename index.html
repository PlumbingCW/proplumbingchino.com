Emergency plumbing

When to call an emergency plumber in Chino

An emergency plumber is worth calling when waiting makes the damage worse. That includes a burst or leaking pipe you cannot stop, sewage coming back up a drain, a water heater pouring water, no water at all, or a smell of gas. A dripping faucet usually can wait for the morning, though a drip that will not shut off may not.

If the problem is active water, shut off the main valve first, then call. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Plumbing in Chino, from the old town to new tracts

Chino was first subdivided in 1887 and incorporated in 1910. It grew up around sugar beets, then dairies, with a dairy boom from the 1950s into the 1980s that made it a leading milk producer in California. Suburban growth followed in the 1970s. That history matters to your plumbing. Homes near downtown, around the Chino Transit Center at 6th Street and Chino Avenue, tend to be older than houses in newer tracts, and older houses are where aging drain lines and corroded supply pipe turn up.

Businesses along Central Avenue, including the Country Fair Shopping Center in ZIP code 91710, and the Chino Spectrum Towne Center, rely on working restrooms, drains and water heaters. The commercial plumbing page explains common business plumbing problems.

Chino sits at the west end of the Inland Empire, bordered by Ontario to the northeast, Pomona to the northwest and Chino Hills to the west. We serve ZIP codes 91708 and 91710 in Chino and neighboring ZIP 91709 in Chino Hills, plus nearby ZIP codes in Ontario (91758, 91761, 91762, 91764), Pomona (91766 to 91769) and Montclair (91763).
