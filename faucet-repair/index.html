Faucet repair in Chino, CA covers every valve and spout in your home, from the kitchen faucet to the angle stop under the sink to the hose bib on the side of the house. A drip costs more than people expect. A faucet losing one drop per second wastes roughly 3,000 gallons a year. A faucet that will not shut off at all is a different matter and may need the supply valve closed right now.

Most faucet problems fall into a few categories: worn seats and washers, failing cartridges, loose handles, corroded supply lines and failed shutoff valves. A plumber can identify which one in minutes.

When a faucet or valve will not shut off

A handle that spins freely, a burst supply line under a sink or a valve that cannot be closed is an emergency. Until a plumber arrives, take these steps.

  1. Close the angle stop under the sink by turning it clockwise. If it will not turn, do not force it.
  2. If the stop fails, close the main water valve at the house or meter.
  3. Open a low faucet to drain pressure, then place a bucket under the leak.
  4. Keep electrical outlets and cords away from the water.
  5. Note the faucet brand and photograph the leak for the plumber.

Leaking handles and spouts

A leak at the handle usually means a worn cartridge or O-ring. Ball, cartridge, ceramic disc and compression faucets each wear differently. Replacing the cartridge is the common repair, and our leaking handle guide shows how it works. Dripping from the spout points to a worn seat or cartridge, and the repair often costs less than a new faucet.

Outdoor faucets

Hose bibs fail at the packing nut, the washer or the stem. Winter damage is less common in Chino's mild climate than in colder places, but a hose left attached can still strain the fitting. Our exterior faucet repair guide covers simple fixes. A bib that leaks inside the wall needs a plumber promptly.

Shower and tub valves

A shower that drips after shutoff has a worn cartridge or seat. Tub faucets can leak behind the wall at the stem, and a hot water leak may appear as a wet ceiling below. These repairs often require opening the wall, so a plumber can advise whether a trim kit or a new valve body is needed.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

The angle stop under a sink and the shutoff behind a toilet are valves you do not use often, which makes them prone to sticking and weeping. Replacing them before an emergency is cheap insurance. Braided supply lines last roughly 5 to 10 years, and an old one can burst. A plumber replaces both during the same visit.

Repair or replace the faucet?

If the faucet is under 10 years old and the body is intact, repair parts are the economical choice. If the finish is flaking, the body is pitted, or parts are no longer made, replacement makes sense. Related fixtures are covered on plumbing installation.
