Our plumbing services page explains each service, and our guides cover common problems and when to stop and call a plumber.

How a call works

You describe the problem, whether it is water on a floor, a drain that will not clear or a heater that went cold. We may ask where you are, what you have already tried and whether anyone is at risk. If the problem is active water, close the main valve first. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911.

We do not ask you to fill out forms. Enquiries happen by phone.

How to check any plumber's license

California requires a contractor license for most plumbing work above a small dollar threshold, and the Contractors State License Board lets anyone look up a license by business name or license number at cslb.ca.gov. The lookup shows whether the license is active, and complaint disclosure information. Plumbers hold the C-36 Plumbing classification. Checking takes two minutes and is worth doing before you let anyone into your home.

This site does not print license numbers or individual plumbers' names. Ask whoever performs the work for their license number, then check it yourself.

How to compare prices

We do not list dollar amounts on this site, because the right number depends on the problem. A clogged sink and a collapsed sewer line are both drain calls, and they are very different jobs. Whoever does the work should diagnose the problem, tell you the price and get your approval before work begins.

If a repair is not worth doing, a good plumber says so. A water heater that has leaked through its tank, or a pipe that has failed in several places, is often better replaced than patched, and the plumber should explain why in plain words.

Emergency and planned work

Planned jobs such as a new water heater, a repipe or a filtration system are easier to compare than an emergency, so ask for the scope, the permit plan and the timing in writing. If you are not sure which kind of job you have, call and describe it.

What to ask any plumber before work starts

Before anyone starts work, ask for the contractor license number and check it at cslb.ca.gov. Ask for proof of liability insurance, ask who will actually do the work, and ask for the scope and the price in writing. A reputable provider expects these questions.

Ask whether a permit is needed and who pulls it. For larger jobs such as a water heater replacement, a repipe or a sewer repair, the City of Chino Building Division can confirm what applies at (909) 334-3251.

If water is running or you smell gas, safety comes first: close the main valve, or leave the building and call the gas utility or 911, and then make the call.

How this site is written

Local facts on this site come from public sources such as the City of Chino General Plan, the City of Chino Building Division and the Contractors State License Board. Where a number can change, such as a permit rule or a water quality report, we point to the source instead of quoting it.

Codes and permits we point to

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, UPC), and permits are issued by the city. In Chino that is the Building Division at 13220 Central Avenue, Chino, CA 91710, phone (909) 334-3251. The division has said that very few kinds of construction or repair are permit-free, so ask it about your job.

We cite codes and standards on our service pages so that you can check them. We do not claim a certification this site cannot back up, and we do not post reviews or awards.
