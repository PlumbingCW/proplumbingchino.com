Sewer line repair in Chino, CA fixes the pipe that carries wastewater from your home to the city sewer or a septic system. When that pipe cracks, shifts, fills with roots or collapses, drains slow down, sewage backs up into low fixtures, and the yard may smell or sink. The repair method depends on what the camera finds, from a short spot repair to a full replacement.

Most homeowners do not know the pipe material or its condition. That is fine.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage rising in a tub, shower or floor drain is an emergency because it carries bacteria and can ruin flooring fast. Do the following while you wait.

  1. Stop all water use, including toilets, sinks, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  2. Keep people and pets away from backed-up areas and wear gloves and boots if you must enter.
  3. Do not pour chemicals into the drains, since they can splash and damage pipe.
  4. Ventilate the room and move belongings off the floor.
  5. Take photos of the backup for your insurer and keep receipts for any cleanup.

Signs your sewer line is failing

Gurgling when water runs, slow drains in several fixtures, sewage odors in the yard, lush green patches over the line, and soil sinking along the pipe path are all signs. Repeated clogs that cables clear only briefly are another. These patterns point away from a single clogged fixture and toward the main line.

The camera inspection decides everything

A sewer camera shows root intrusion, offset joints, cracks, sags where water pools and collapses. It also measures how far from the cleanout the problem sits. The recording is evidence for your repair decision and for any insurance claim. Our sewer inspection page describes what to expect.

Repair options: spot repair, lining or replacement

A localized crack or a short root-damaged section can often be fixed with a spot repair, where the plumber opens the ground over one segment and replaces it. Trenchless lining threads a resin-soaked liner through the existing pipe and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one, which avoids long trenches. Full replacement is the answer when the pipe has collapsed, sags badly or runs under places a trench can reach.

Each method has trade-offs. Lining leaves the pipe slightly smaller and cannot fix a collapsed line. Trenching is more disruptive but fully renews the run. A plumber should explain why one fits your line and not just recommend the pricier choice.

Cast iron, clay and plastic pipe

Older homes may have cast iron or clay sewer pipe, which can crack or scale with age, while newer homes use plastic. Cast iron stacks inside walls and slabs can corrode from inside, as our cast iron sewer pipe guide explains. Cast iron section replacement is common in older construction.

Chino was first subdivided in 1887 and had a long dairy history, so the housing stock ranges from old downtown lots to postwar tracts to recent development. Your home's age is a better guide to its pipe than the neighborhood name.

Septic and private systems

Some properties in the Chino area are on septic systems rather than a city sewer. The City of Chino Building Division lists on-site wastewater disposal standards, which apply to septic work. If you are unsure whether you have a septic tank, check your property records or call the division at (909) 334-3251.
