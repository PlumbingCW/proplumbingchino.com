Garbage disposal repair in Chino, CA covers jams, humming motors, leaks from the housing or the sink flange, slow drains and noisy grinding. A disposal is a small motor with grinding plates, and it works under harsh conditions. Fixing a jam takes minutes. A leaking housing means replacement. Knowing which you have saves money.

Plumbers also install new disposals, reconnect dishwasher drain lines and fix the sink drain that feeds them. If the disposal and the sink drain are both slow, the clog may sit beyond the disposal, in the trap arm or the wall stub.

When a disposal problem is an emergency

Water pouring from the unit, a burning smell or a sink that is overflowing onto the cabinet floor is urgent. Before the plumber arrives, do the following.

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall switch, and unplug it under the sink if it plugs in.
  2. Never put your hand in the unit. Use tongs or pliers to remove visible objects after the power is off.
  3. Stop running water into the sink. Close the supply valves if the cabinet is flooding.
  4. Place a bucket under any leak and empty the cabinet of cleaning products.
  5. Check the reset button on the bottom of the unit for a tripped overload.

Humming and jammed disposals

If the unit hums but does not spin, something is stuck between the grinding plate and the shredder. With power off, a hex wrench in the socket at the bottom of the unit turns the plate back and forth to free it. Then press the red reset button. If it jams again right away, the motor may be failing or bearings worn.

Leaks: three places to look

Check the sink flange at the top, the dishwasher inlet on the side and the housing seam at the bottom. A leak at the flange needs a new gasket or putty. A loose dishwasher hose clamp is an easy fix. A leak from the housing seam means the unit is done. Our faucet repair page covers leaks from the tap above.

Slow drains past the disposal

Because the disposal sits at the front of the drain, people blame it for every clog. Grease coats the pipe past the unit, and the trap and wall stub catch solids. A plumber cables the line from the trap to clear it. If the problem recurs, see drain cleaning and hydro jetting.

Replacing and installing

Choose horsepower by use. A half-horsepower unit handles light use, three quarters to one horsepower suits larger families. Installation requires mounting the flange, connecting the drain, wiring or a plug connection, and the dishwasher inlet. A plumber also checks that the drain slope is correct and the trap is not clogged.

What not to grind

Skip grease, coffee grounds in bulk, fibrous vegetables like celery and corn husks, bones and starchy foods like pasta and rice, which swell. Run cold water during and after use for about 15 seconds. These habits extend the life of the unit and the drain.

Safety steps before you touch a jammed disposal

Do not put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Switch it off at the wall and unplug it or turn off the breaker before you use the hex key from below. Never use chemical cleaners in a jammed unit, since they splash during repair.

Disposal repair often goes hand in hand with a slow kitchen line. When both are involved, the plumber can address them in one visit.

Disposal drains connect through a trap and, where the code requires, an air gap or high loop for the dishwasher line.
