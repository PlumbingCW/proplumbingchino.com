A plumber in The Preserve, Chino, CA gets called for the same problems as anywhere else: a leak, a slow drain, a water heater that stopped heating. What is different here is the setting. The Preserve is a master-planned area of newer homes, townhomes and condominiums, and many of them sit inside a homeowners association. That changes who you call first and what you check before any repair starts.

If water is actively leaking, close the main valve and call (909) 505-3225. Tell us what is happening and we will explain what service is available for your address.

Where The Preserve sits and how it was built

The City of Chino annexed this area in 2003, and the city adopted The Preserve Specific Plan on March 25, 2003 (Ordinance 2003-06). The plan area lies south of Pine Avenue. Euclid Avenue and part of State Route 71 form its western edge, Kimball and Merrill Avenues mark much of the northern line, and the Prado Flood Control Basin lies to the south.

The land was once part of the Chino agricultural preserve, and the city's 2045 General Plan update describes the area as about 60 percent built out. That matters if you live here: new streets and homes are still being finished, so ask the builder or the HOA whether any planned utility work affects your street.

Newer does not mean maintenance free

The specific plan allows everything from estate lots to apartments, so one street can mix single-family homes, townhomes and condominiums. Each type fails in its own way. A townhome has shared walls, so a leak can reach a neighbor. A detached home has its own water heater, often in the garage or a side yard.

Water heaters, fill valves, supply lines and garbage disposals wear out on their own schedule, whatever the year of the house. If a part fails, a plumber can tell you whether it is a quick repair or a replacement. Our water heater repair page explains what each symptom usually means.

Warranty and HOA questions before the repair

If the home is still under a builder warranty, read it before you authorize outside work. Some warranties set conditions on who may make repairs, and the builder can tell you what they require. Keep photos, receipts and the plumber's written findings either way.

In a townhome or condominium, the HOA often owns the common lines and sometimes the walls around them. Your declaration of covenants says where your responsibility starts. A plumber can find what is failing, and your HOA documents say who pays for it.

Common jobs in this area

Most calls are for leak detection when a bill climbs or a wall feels damp, drain cleaning for kitchen and bathroom clogs, and tankless water heater service for units that need a flush or a repair. Homeowners also ask about plumbing installation for new fixtures and softener or filter hookups.

Larger work such as a water heater replacement generally needs a permit. The City of Chino Building Division is at 13220 Central Avenue and can be reached at (909) 334-3251.
