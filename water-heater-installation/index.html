Choosing between tank, tankless and heat pump

Tank heaters store 30 to 80 gallons and keep it hot all day. They cost the least to buy and are the easiest to install. Tankless units heat water as it flows, so there is no standby loss and no tank to leak, but they need a properly sized gas line or a high-amperage electric circuit. Heat pump units pull heat from the air and cut electricity use substantially, at a higher purchase price.

The right choice is usually set by the house, not by a brochure. A garage with a gas line suits a gas tank. A closet in a condo may only allow electric. A tight space with no airflow rules out a heat pump.

Where the heater goes

Location drives labor. Moving a heater from a garage to a side yard means new gas and water runs, and an attic install needs a pan, a drain line and safe access for future service. Putting the new unit where the old one stood is the cheapest option because connections already exist.

A relief valve discharge line must run downhill to a safe termination point. Plumbers set that up on every install, and the City of Chino Building Division, at 13220 Central Avenue, publishes a water heater strapping handout that applies to how tanks are secured.

Expansion tanks, pressure and water quality

Heating water makes it expand. In a home with a check valve or pressure reducing valve, that pressure has nowhere to go, and it can stress fittings and shorten the heater's life. An expansion tank gives the water room to grow. A plumber can test your static pressure with a gauge during the visit.

Hard water leaves scale on elements and tank bottoms. Ask your water provider for its annual water quality report, or test your tap, to see how hard your water is. Our water softener and filtration page covers treatment options.

Gas safety on new installs

Gas heaters need proper combustion air, venting and a leak-tight gas connection. After the install, the plumber checks connections for leaks and confirms the flue draws correctly. If you ever smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.
