Tankless Water Heater Filter Replacement and Flush in Chino, CA

Most tankless water heaters have a small inlet screen and a heat exchanger that scales up over time. Cleaning both is basic upkeep that keeps hot water steady.

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so it has less margin than a tank when something restricts the flow. Two things most often cut performance. A clogged inlet filter starves the unit of water, and scale inside the heat exchanger makes it run hot and trip an error code. Both are maintenance items, not signs the unit is dead.

This guide covers the inlet filter and the vinegar flush that most manufacturers describe. Always read your own manual first. Models differ, and a step that is fine on one brand can void the warranty on another.

Signs the filter or heat exchanger needs attention

Hot water that fades from hot to lukewarm while a shower runs, a flow that seems weaker at the hot side only, or a unit that shows an error code for water flow or temperature all point to a restriction. If cold water is just as weak, the problem is likely at the house supply rather than the heater.

Another sign is a heater that has never been serviced. Many manufacturers recommend yearly descaling in hard water areas and every few years elsewhere. A unit that is five years old with no service history is a good candidate for both steps.

Clean the inlet filter

Turn off power to the unit, shut the cold water supply and open a hot tap to relieve pressure. Place a towel and a shallow pan under the cold inlet. Unscrew the filter housing or cap and pull out the screen, which may be a small cylinder or a mesh disc.

Rinse it under running water and brush gently with a soft toothbrush. Check for grit, bits of pipe scale and white mineral flakes. Reinstall it with its gasket seated, hand tighten the cap and open the cold supply slowly. Run a hot tap until the air clears.

Flush the scale with vinegar

A flush needs isolation valves on the hot and cold lines, which many installers put in. If yours has none, a plumber can add a service valve kit, and it makes every future flush a ten minute job. With the valves, you connect two hoses to the service ports, run the cold side into a bucket with white vinegar, and circulate it through the unit with a small submersible pump.

Circulate for 45 minutes to an hour, then rinse with clean water until the vinegar smell is gone. Close the ports, reopen the valves and check for leaks. The mineral flakes in the bucket tell you how much scale you removed.

Reduce scale in the first place

Scale forms from minerals dissolved in water. A softener or a scale-reducing filter ahead of the heater slows it. Setting the output temperature no higher than needed also helps, because scale forms faster at higher temperatures. Our water softener and filtration page explains the options.

When a plumber should do it

Call a plumber if the unit still throws a code after cleaning, if you cannot find isolation valves, or if you see water at the gas or vent connections. Gas tankless units also need correct venting, and a failing vent can be dangerous. A slow flush on a heavily scaled unit can take more than one pass. The tankless water heater page covers installation and service, and water heater repair covers diagnosis.

A simple yearly checklist

Set a reminder once a year and run through the same few items. Clean the inlet filter, flush the heat exchanger if your water is hard, check the vent and condensate line for blockage, and look at every connection for weeping. Write the date and the result on a label near the unit.

Keep the manual and the model number with that label. A plumber or a manufacturer's support line can answer questions much faster when the model number is in hand, and warranty claims often ask for proof of regular upkeep.

Error codes worth knowing about

Codes differ by brand, but flow, ignition and temperature codes are the most common. A flow code usually means a clogged filter or a closed valve. An ignition code can mean a gas supply issue. A temperature code often follows scale. Look up the code in your manual before you reset the unit more than once.
