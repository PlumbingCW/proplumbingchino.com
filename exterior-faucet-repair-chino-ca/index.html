How to Repair a Leaking Exterior Faucet in Chino, CA

An outdoor faucet that drips or sprays from the handle is usually a worn packing washer. A leak at the wall is a different problem and needs more care.

The exterior faucet, also called a hose bib or sillcock, takes more abuse than any faucet inside the house. It sits in sun, gets yanked on by hoses and is forgotten for months at a time. A drip can waste a surprising amount of water, and a leak inside the wall can rot framing before you see it.

The first step is to work out where the water comes from. A leak from the spout, the handle or the nut under the handle is usually a quick repair. A leak where the faucet meets the wall, or a damp spot indoors on the other side, is a plumbing leak that deserves a professional look.

Find where it leaks

Dry the faucet with a rag and open it a little. If water seeps from behind the handle, the packing nut or packing washer is worn. If it drips from the spout when the faucet is closed, the seat washer is worn. If water appears at the wall behind the faucet, the pipe or the faucet body is leaking inside the wall.

Look indoors as well. On the other side of the wall, check for staining, soft drywall or a musty smell. A hidden leak at a hose bib is often found only when someone checks the inside of the wall.

Fix a leak at the handle

Close the supply to the faucet if there is a shutoff valve, or shut the main water valve. Open the faucet to drain it. Remove the handle screw and pull the handle off. Use a wrench to snug the packing nut a quarter turn. If it still leaks, unscrew the packing nut and replace the packing washer or O-ring with an exact match.

Coat the new washer lightly with plumber's grease, reassemble and turn the water back on slowly. Many handle leaks stop with a packing washer that costs very little.

Fix a drip from the spout

A faucet that drips when closed has a worn seat washer at the end of the stem. Remove the stem, take off the screw at the end and replace the washer. Look at the brass seat in the faucet body. If it is rough, a seat dressing tool can smooth it, or the faucet may need replacing.

A frost-free faucet has a long stem and the washer is deep inside the pipe. Pull the full stem out to reach it, and take the old one to the store to match it.

Vacuum breakers and hose use

Many hose bibs include a vacuum breaker, a small cap on the spout that stops water from siphoning back into the house supply. If it spits water from the cap, the breaker may be worn and can be replaced separately. Leave the hose attached only when you are using it, because a hose left under pressure strains the bib and can freeze in a cold snap.

When to replace the whole faucet

Replace the faucet when the body is corroded, the stem is seized, or the leak is at the wall. A replacement can mean cutting and sweating copper or working with PEX, which is plumber's work for most people. See our faucet repair page for what a visit covers, and our leak detection page if you suspect water inside the wall.

Parts and tools to keep on hand

A small assortment of packing washers, seat washers, O-rings and a spare vacuum breaker cap covers most outdoor faucet repairs. A packing wrench or an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver and plumber's grease finish the kit. Take a photo of the faucet before you remove it, because outdoor faucet bodies come in many shapes and the picture helps at the supply counter.

Signs the leak is inside the wall

Water that runs down the siding below the faucet, a patch of wet stucco, or a hose bib that drips only after you use it can all signal a split in the stem pipe behind the wall. Mildew on the interior wall, or a swollen baseboard, adds to the case. Stop using the faucet and shut off its valve until it is checked.

Outdoor faucets in Chino's climate

Chino has a warm Inland Empire climate, so deep freezes are uncommon, but a cold night can still reach freezing in some years. A hose left attached overnight is the usual reason an outdoor faucet cracks. Disconnect hoses in cold weather and consider a foam cover.

Water use matters in a state that has been through repeated droughts. The City of Chino Hills posts water information through its annual report at chinohills.org/CCR, and Chino residents can ask their own provider for the equivalent. For west side addresses, see our Chino Hills page.
