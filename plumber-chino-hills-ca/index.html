Chino Hills lies directly west of Chino in San Bernardino County, and Chino Pro Plumbing's enquiries come from nearby Chino.

The city was incorporated on December 1, 1991, with about 42,000 residents at the time. By the 2020 census it had grown to 78,411. That quick growth shapes the plumbing here, because most of the housing stock was built in the same window and ages on the same schedule.

Homes from the 1980s and early 1990s: what ages first

Chino Hills stayed largely rural, with horse properties and dairies, until the mid-1970s. Rapid suburban development followed in the 1980s and early 1990s, and many neighborhoods use a village layout with parks and shopping close by. Master-planned areas include Vellano, Los Serranos, Woodview and Village Crossing, Gordon Ranch, LaBand Village, Butterfield Ranch, Rolling Ridge, Fairfield Ranch and Payne Ranch.

A house built in that period is now 30 to 40 years old. That is the age when original water heaters have usually been replaced once, supply fittings begin to fail, and shutoff valves under sinks seize. Some builders in the late 1970s to mid-1990s used polybutylene supply pipe, so if you have gray or blue plastic pipe in the garage or at the water heater, mention it when you call. Pipe that age often justifies a look at repiping before a pinhole leak makes the decision for you.

Hills, pressure and shutoff valves

The city sits in rolling hills, with undeveloped hills along its western edge and an elevation near 860 feet. Streets that drop from one tract to the next can mean noticeably different water pressure from house to house. A gauge on an outdoor spigot shows your static pressure, and numbers above roughly 80 psi are hard on fixtures, water heaters and supply lines. A pressure reducing valve is a small fix that prevents big failures, covered in plumbing installation.

Chino Hills also sits in earthquake country. The Chino fault runs along the city's eastern edge and the Whittier fault along the west, and a magnitude 5.4 earthquake in 2008 was attributed to them. Know where your main water valve is, check that your water heater is strapped, and ask about flexible connectors so a shake does not become a flood. Our water heater installation page covers strapping and connectors.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

1. Shut off the main valve

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. Locate it before you need it, near the street side or the meter.

2. Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off the breaker only if you can reach it dry and safely.

3. Clear the area

Move rugs, electronics and valuables away from the water. Keep children and pets out of wet rooms.

Where Chino Hills water comes from

The City of Chino Hills supplies its own drinking water. Its annual Consumer Confidence Report says the supply comes from groundwater pumped from City-owned wells, Monte Vista Water District wells and Chino Basin Desalter Authority wells, plus imported State Water Project water delivered by the Metropolitan Water District. Recycled water comes from the Inland Empire Utilities Agency.

The city's Water Quality Technician takes questions about the report at (909) 364-2808, and the full report is at chinohills.org/CCR. We do not quote a hardness number for Chino Hills here, because the report is the right source. If scale builds on fixtures or in the water heater, read it and see our water softener and filtration page.
