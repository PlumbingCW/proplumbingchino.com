What College Park is and where it sits

The City of Chino General Plan describes College Park as a large master-planned residential area in the south of the city. It includes Ayala Park, and Chaffey College's Chino campus sits next to the park. The neighborhood was built on land that had been held in an agricultural preserve and on surplus State land.

Builders marketed smaller sections inside the plan under their own names, such as Hampshire, Emerson and Hillsdale at College Park. If your paperwork uses one of those names, the plumbing advice here still applies. Homes in this area use ZIP code 91710.

Maintenance checkpoints at the two decade mark

The College Park Specific Plan dates from the early 2000s, so many original homes are now around twenty years old. A tank water heater commonly serves 8 to 12 years, so an original tank in a home from that period is well past the usual range. If you still have the original, ask a plumber to check it before it fails.

The same logic applies to parts that wear: toilet fill valves, faucet cartridges, braided supply lines and garbage disposals. None of this means your home has a problem. It means a short inspection is cheaper than a repair after a failure. See our notes on water heater replacement for how the decision usually goes.

Small yards, irrigation and the street shutoff

Compact lots mean the water meter, the main shutoff and the irrigation controller are all close to the house. Learn where each one is. A broken sprinkler head or a cracked hose bib can run for days without being noticed, and the first sign is often a higher bill.

If your bill jumps with no change in habits, shut off every fixture and check whether the meter still moves. A moving meter points to a leak, and leak detection finds it without opening walls. For dripping or failing fixtures, see faucet repair and toilet repair.

What the association covers and what you do

Where College Park sections have an HOA, the clubhouse, pool and common areas are the association's to maintain. Your own lot, fixtures and water heater are normally yours. Your declaration of covenants spells out the line, so check it before assuming either way.

For stubborn clogs in kitchen and bathroom lines, drain cleaning clears the pipe, and a camera check shows whether something else is wrong.

Before you call from College Park

1. Find the meter and main

Know where the meter box and the main shutoff are on your lot.

2. Check the water heater age

Look for a manufacture date on the label, usually near the serial number.

3. Turn off irrigation

If wet ground is the problem, shut off the sprinkler controller and the supply valve.

4. Ask your HOA

If the problem touches a shared line or wall, tell the association first.
