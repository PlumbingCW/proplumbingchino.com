Kitchen Island Plumbing and Venting in Chino, CA

A sink in a kitchen island looks simple, but there is no wall behind it for a vent pipe. The usual answer is an island loop vent, and it has strict rules.

A kitchen island with a sink is one of the most requested remodel features, and one of the trickiest plumbing jobs in a kitchen. A normal sink drain connects to a vent pipe in the wall behind it. An island has no wall, so the plumber has to find another way to bring air to the drain.

The standard answer is the island loop vent. The pipe rises from the drain under the island, loops up to just under the counter height, then drops through the floor and runs to a vent in a nearby wall. It looks strange on the plan, but it follows a rule in the code.

Why the vent matters

Without a vent, water leaving the sink pulls air through the trap and sucks the water seal out. That lets sewer gas into the kitchen, and the sink gurgles and drains slowly. The vent keeps the pressure balanced so the water flows and the trap stays full.

An island with a dishwasher or a disposal adds to the demand, because each of them pushes more water down the same line at once.

How an island loop vent works

The vent pipe leaves the drain just below the trap arm, climbs to a point just under the underside of the countertop and then turns down toward the floor. It then travels beneath the floor to the nearest wall and connects to a vent stack. The high point of the loop is what prevents drain water from flowing into the vent.

The loop has to be sized, sloped and supported the way the code directs. A plumber lays it out so that it stays accessible and does not weaken the floor framing.

Other options

An air admittance valve can sometimes serve an island, but acceptance varies by city and the valve must remain accessible. A re-vent through a nearby wall is another method. Some jurisdictions allow a wet vent when the layout suits. Check with the building department before you plan your cabinets.

Slope, access and the floor

A drain has to slope toward the stack at a set rate, typically a quarter inch per foot for small pipe, so distance matters. A long run from an island to a wall may need lowered floor framing or a thicker floor build up. Ask your plumber for the exact slope and fixture arrangement before the cabinets are ordered.

Water supply lines, power for a disposal and a dishwasher drain loop add to the under-floor traffic. Planning all of them together prevents costly changes after demolition.

Permits and hiring

Moving a sink to an island is a permitted job in most places and gets inspected before the walls and floor are closed. Hire a licensed plumber with island experience. Our plumbing installation page covers new fixtures, and our drain cleaning page covers clearing slow lines in a finished kitchen.

Plan the sink and the dishwasher together

An island sink often shares its drain with a dishwasher and a disposal, and each of them has its own connection rules. The dishwasher drain needs a high loop or an air gap to stop dirty water from flowing back. The disposal needs power and a dedicated switch. Decide where each will go before the floor is opened, so the plumber and the electrician can set their lines in one visit.

Take photos of the open floor before it is closed. Years later, when you need to find a pipe or a junction, those photos save a lot of guesswork.

Island sinks in Chino remodels

Remodels in Chino often open kitchens that were built in the tract decades since the 1970s. Slab foundations are common in newer tracts, so an island sink may need a trench cut in the slab to run the drain. That is a significant decision, so ask your plumber about the floor type before you design the kitchen.

The City of Chino Building Division, at 13220 Central Avenue, Chino, CA 91710 and (909) 334-3251, issues plumbing permits, and the inspector will review the island vent. For west side homes, see our Chino Hills page.

The island vent rules sit in the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
