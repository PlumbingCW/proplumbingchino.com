Do Tankless Water Heaters Save Money in Chino, CA?

A tankless water heater can use less energy than a tank, but the savings depend on how much hot water your household uses and what the install requires.

A tank water heater keeps thirty to eighty gallons hot all day and night, whether anyone needs it or not. Some of that heat leaks through the tank walls into the garage, which the industry calls standby loss. A tankless heater heats water only when a tap is open, so it has no standby loss to speak of.

That is the basis for the savings claim. The question for your household is how large the savings are against what the unit costs and what the install demands. For some homes the answer is clearly yes. For others the payback is long enough that a good tank heater is the better choice.

Where the energy savings come from

According to the U.S. Department of Energy, gas tankless heaters can be about 24 to 34 percent more energy efficient than a conventional storage tank. That applies to homes that use around 41 gallons or less of hot water a day. For homes that use a lot, around 86 gallons a day, the gain is about 8 to 14 percent. The gain shrinks as usage rises, because a busy household keeps a tank from sitting idle.

The savings are not just a number on the box. Tankless units with condensing technology reclaim more heat from exhaust, which raises efficiency further. Their ratings use a uniform energy factor, which makes comparing models easier.

What raises the upfront cost

Tankless units cost more than tanks, and installation often costs more too. A gas model needs a larger gas line to feed its high burner demand, special venting made of stainless steel or approved plastic, a condensate drain for condensing models and sometimes an electrical outlet nearby. Retrofitting these into a garage built for a tank can take real work.

Hard water adds a maintenance cost. Scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and yearly flushing keeps the unit working. Our tankless water heater page covers installation and service.

Lifespan and maintenance

Manufacturers often rate tankless units for twenty years or more with maintenance, compared with roughly eight to twelve for a tank. A longer life offsets part of the higher price. The rating assumes yearly flushing and an inlet filter check. Skip those and the life shortens.

Who benefits most

A household with moderate hot water use, a family that runs out of hot water on a tank, or a homeowner short on garage space often gains the most. Endless hot water for back to back showers is the comfort benefit. A very large household may need two units to keep up with simultaneous demand.

If your current tank is working and your use is heavy, replacing it with a good tank unit may beat tankless on total cost.

Make the decision with numbers

Ask your plumber for installed quotes for both a tank and a tankless unit at your address, with the gas, venting and electrical work itemized. Add the maintenance and the expected life to compare. Our water heater installation page describes the process, and our water heater replacement page covers swapping a failed tank.

Rebates and incentives

Utilities and the state sometimes offer rebates for high efficiency water heaters, and the programs change from year to year. Ask your gas and electric provider what is available for your address before you buy, and ask your plumber whether the model you are considering qualifies. Rebates can shorten the payback on a higher priced unit.

Tankless water heaters in Chino homes

Chino's climate is mild, so incoming water is not as cold in winter as in colder states, which helps tankless output. Many homes here have the water heater in a garage, where venting is easier to route, though older garages may have limited gas line capacity.
